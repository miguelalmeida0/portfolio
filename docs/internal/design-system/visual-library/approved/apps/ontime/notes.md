# Ontime

## Status
Approved app reference

## What Migi likes
- Social coordination that feels low-pressure and human instead of corporate scheduling.
- Friend availability, status dots, distance/time cues, and plan momentum shown as visible product state.
- Warm pastel cards, chunky readable type, and direct action language.
- Modal and selection flows that make a social action feel small, reversible, and easy.

## What future agents should copy
- For social products, show who is available and what action will happen next.
- Use copy to lower pressure and clarify social stakes.
- Make live state, forming state, and invite state visually distinct.
- Keep primary social actions large and obvious while preserving friend context.

## What future agents must not copy blindly
- Do not copy exact friend names, photos, locations, plan copy, or social assumptions.
- Do not use fake distance, availability, or live-state claims.
- Do not apply the warm pastel palette to unrelated products.
- Do not treat the bottom-left capture/control artifact as product UI.

## Scope
Global lessons for social coordination and app-specific tone/color choices.

## Top 10 Reusable Lessons

1. Lead with the product object, person, or scene before adding panels and metadata.
2. Pair browse/selection states with detail states so future agents understand the full product rhythm.
3. Keep status badges, counters, and telemetry secondary to the main visual read.
4. Use translucent or glass surfaces only when they remain readable over the background.
5. Let product category shape the UI material: terracotta for tactile commerce, blue space for travel, dark neon for mobility, warm orange for wellness.
6. Show state progression when the product is a journey, such as waiting, playing, arriving, unlocking, or inspecting.
7. Use one dominant accent color to signal action or active status rather than decorating every element.
8. Keep dashboards specific to a real operational loop; dense cards work only when each module has a clear job.
9. Make bottom navigation and floating docks quiet enough that they do not hide content.
10. Use hero imagery as functional context, not a generic atmospheric background.

## Top 10 Things Agents Must Avoid

1. Do not use these screenshots as production assets.
2. Do not copy brands, people, product names, icons, destination imagery, or exact copy.
3. Do not reproduce watermarks, browser overlays, source-site controls, device chrome, or cropped capture artifacts.
4. Do not invent live states, follower counts, ratings, location data, battery levels, salaries, security scores, or AI generation claims.
5. Do not turn every visual-heavy task into a generic SaaS dashboard.
6. Do not force a one-color palette when the product needs comparison, contrast, or accessibility.
7. Do not use glassmorphism on busy imagery without screenshot QA for contrast and overlap.
8. Do not add product logic before the visual shell and core loop are understood.
9. Do not copy literal subject matter unless Migi explicitly requests Literal Target Copy Mode.
10. Do not treat desktop travel references as mobile app patterns without adapting layout and interaction density.

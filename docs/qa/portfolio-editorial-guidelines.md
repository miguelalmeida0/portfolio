# Portfolio voice and typography

Write as Miguel would explain the work in an interview. Use existing public facts, first person for personal contributions and team language for shared delivery. A simple question can have a short answer. Do not invent a personality, mood, lesson, metric or experience to make an answer more interesting.

Lead each project with what it does and what Miguel built. Use ordinary verbs: built, designed, checked, loaded, changed, kept. Explain the consequence of an engineering choice before naming the mechanism. Keep exact technical terms in the section that explains them. Retain useful numbers, dates, limits and evidence conditions.

Avoid slogan-style pairs, repeated three-part commands, poetic metaphors and self-awarded qualities. Every paragraph should add information. Show reliability through a concrete example: a failed request keeps the draft; an older result cannot replace a newer query. Do not replace a useful explanation with an adjective such as calm, thoughtful, seamless, premium or powerful.

Labels describe their destinations: Try the case demo opens the prepared example; Open live app opens the external product; Code opens the repository. Disclose prepared answers, fictional screens and controlled speech where they are used. Date historical checks and never turn one successful sample into a reliability claim. A copy button copies the text the visitor sees, or states exactly what else it copies.

Keep Big Shoulders Display for the portrait hero and Antonio for established display roles. Figtree carries reading and conversational text. Preserve product typography inside screenshots and interactive artifacts. Give metadata/utility text a clear job rather than adding a decorative typeface. Check actual physical reading size under the desktop scale. Do not add handwritten type or labels. Font families alone do not indicate authorship or quality.

Check each edited page at 320,390,768,1024 and1440 pixels. A lack of document overflow does not prove that labels fit: inspect project names, action groups, focus and touch targets. Keep functional controls usable before and after an animation, and respect the shared motion policy.

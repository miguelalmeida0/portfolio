    Interpret first. Earn the right to commit.
    
      
    Interpretation proposes operations. Resolution identifies their targets. Deterministic checks decide whether a candidate document is safe to return.

    The engine clones the document, applies actions to a draft and validates the result. It returns a successful document or a clarification, confirmation or conflict. The caller owns the next transition.
    

    
    
      
    
        
    A scheduling operation could lose an event.
    “Move lunch after standup and protect my workout.”
    
        
    
          
    Observed
    During arrangeDay, flexible events could disappear. In compound commands, a second failure could leave the first operation applied.
    
          
    Cause
    The early path coupled interpretation to mutation. Demo-specific event IDs and seeded times hid weaknesses against user-created data.
    
          
    Change
    Resolve references, clone the document, apply actions to the draft, then validate. A failure returns before the candidate becomes authoritative. Identity, relationships and protected anchors are constraints on the operation.
    
          
    Evidence
    Source tests cover rollback on an invalid relationship, duplicate identities, stable linked-event identity and atomic moves across dates.
    
          
    Tradeoff
    Atomic rejection can turn a partly useful request into no change at all. A clarification is easier to understand than an invisible half-success.

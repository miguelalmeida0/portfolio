# Domain Palette Fit Guidance

Palette must fit the product domain, user environment, task pressure, and emotional state.

## Rule

Do not select colors because they "look cool" in isolation. The color system must support the domain:

- Hospitality operations: warm enough for human service, clear enough for pressure, with state colors that map to tables, delays, reservations, kitchen status, and handoffs.
- Healthcare / safety: calm, legible, non-alarming except for true urgent state.
- Finance / operations: precise, stable, high-contrast, stateful, and not playful unless the product calls for it.
- Creative / editorial: expressive palettes are allowed, but contrast and concept fit still matter.
- Mobile utilities: tactile, readable, and tied to the task context.

## Required Questions

- What physical or digital environment will the user be in?
- Is the task calm, urgent, repetitive, emotional, analytical, or social?
- What colors carry semantic state in this domain?
- What colors would feel fake, random, theatrical, or imported from another product type?
- Does the palette help the primary object/action become clearer?

## Rejected

- Random dark palette for a warm human-service domain.
- "Restaurant" palette made only from brown/orange without reason.
- Muddy sepia/espresso/umber brown-black archive wash used as a background, stage, card, panel, container, sidebar, or navigation color.
- Nocturne, archive, field-guide, botanical, brass, tobacco, parchment, vintage, cinematic, or premium language used to justify that same banned brown-black surface family.
- Terminal green or neon tech palettes for non-technical operations.
- AI purple/blue glow as default identity.
- Palette chosen only because it matched a screenshot mood, not product use.
